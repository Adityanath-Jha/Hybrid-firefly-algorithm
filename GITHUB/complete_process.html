Energy-aware, communication-constrained Firefly swarm for search and rescue

How one generation works

Each call of step() moves the world forward once. The order matters, because every stage uses the result of the one before it.

1

Move the world

Survivors drift or stand; hazards grow, move and slide around the base and standing survivors.

2

Pick the phase and radio range

Static survivors first; drifters from generation 60. Radio range may drop to 40 %. γ = ln 2 / Rc².

3

Assign one target per drone

Each drone knows what it or a radio mate senses. Greedy nearest pairing in rounds, up to a quota of 3 guards.

4

Battery relay

Low drones return, docked ones recharge, standby drones relaunch to keep the fleet at strength.

5

Compute brightness

I = 100/(1+dtarget) − 0.075·dbase − 500·[in hazard].

6

Build and take the move

Firefly pull + separation + pursuit (or exploration) + jitter, then a hazard-safe step limited to 0.45.

7

Mark survivors

A standing survivor with an active drone within 0.55 is marked. When all are marked, the swarm returns to base.

Design principles

One drone, one target · local knowledge only · forces add up and are capped · safety is a hard rule · energy is first class.

The algorithm in detail

The mathematics behind each stage, with the lines of FA_final.py that implement it. The full derivations are in the report.

Perception and communication lines 255–261, 317–333

Ki ∋ k ⇔ minj∈Ni ‖pj − sk‖ < Rs

Drone i knows survivor k if it, or any active drone within the radio range, is closer than the sensing range 4.0. This is the communication constraint: a drone with no radio mate near a survivor learns nothing and explores.

Target assignment lines 257, 329–333

q = max(1, min(3, ⌊nact / nunm⌋))

Greedy rounds: round r takes the closest free (drone, survivor) pair among survivors that already have r guards, until the quota q is reached. Leftover drones explore.

Firefly brightness lines 267–273

Ii = 100 / (1 + diτ) − we·0.15·dib − 500·𝟙[pi ∈ Z]

A proximity score, a small energy term and a heavy hazard penalty. Dead, non-active or escaping drones get −109 so that nobody follows them.

Attraction lines 279–286

βij = ½ β0 e−γ r², γ = ln 2 / Rc², ‖Fi‖ ≤ 1.0

Only brighter drones on the same target within Rc attract. Because γ is tied to Rc, the attraction at the edge of the radio range is always half of its value at zero distance.

Movement law lines 287–307

v = F + S + G + J (with target) · v = S + C + W + J (explorer)

F Firefly pull (capped 1.0), S separation (0.9 within 0.8), G pursuit (1.35 static, 1.05 moving), C cover push (1.6 within 2.2), W waypoint pull 0.75, J jitter α = 0.25. The sum is limited to the step Δ = 0.45.

Hazard-safe motion lines 115–166

Frep = 3.6 · ((rd − d) / (rd − ρ))², rd = ρ + 0.28 + 1.35

A potential field with a sideways swerve, a hard projection out of every hazard, a stuck nudge, and a stagnation escape that detects a drone going nowhere for 20 generations.

Energy and relay lines 167–231

return when E < 2·dbase + 16 + 2·|Z|; recharge +6 per generation

Flying costs 1.5 per unit. A returning drone docks within 0.40 of the base, recharges, goes on standby, and relaunches towards the least-covered survivor.

Marking and phases lines 258–261, 310–314

mark ⇔ ∃ active i: ‖pi − sk‖ < 0.55 ∧ k standing

Static survivors are served first. Drifters are tracked from generation 60 (or earlier when no standing survivor is left), and can only be marked once they stop. When every survivor is marked, the swarm flies home.

What does and does not matter


Matters: target assignment, the pursuit force, the battery relay and the sensing range (Rs 4.0 → 1.5 raises the slowest discovery from 33 to about 170 generations).


Little measurable effect in the tested scenarios: the radio range (1 to 10), the Firefly pull (β0 = 1 against 0) and the energy weight (0 to 1.5). The report states this plainly.
